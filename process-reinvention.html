Framework · Process reinvention

From Hours Saved to Value Captured

Most AI programs save hours on paper and capture none of the value, because the process around the AI stays the same. This is how I redesign the work itself, redeploy the capacity AI frees up, and measure what the business actually gains.

The value ladder

AI value comes in four steps. Most programs stop at the first one, report the hours saved, and wonder later why the business results never arrived.

1
Automate the taskAI does an existing step faster. Hours are saved, but the process, roles and decisions are unchanged. Most programs stop here.
2
Redesign the processRemove steps that only existed to move information. Fix data at the source. Change who decides, and when.
3
Redeploy the capacityDecide, explicitly and up front, where the freed time goes. Capacity that isn't redeployed gets absorbed and disappears.
4
Measure the business resultTrack the metric the work was meant to move, such as cycle time, throughput or new initiatives delivered, not just hours saved.

The rule I apply: efficiency that isn't redeployed is a Value Risk. I log it in the RAID log and track it like any other risk, with an owner and a plan.

Five questions I ask before automating anything

  1. Where does the work wait? Hand-offs and approval queues usually cost more time than the work itself.
  2. Which steps exist only to move information? Copying, reformatting and re-entering data are candidates for removal, not automation.
  3. How many places hold the same information? Every duplicate source is a reconciliation job and a chance for the numbers to disagree.
  4. Who needs to decide, and when? If leaders can see the information whenever they want, meetings can be for decisions, not updates.
  5. What will the freed capacity do, and what metric proves it worked? If nobody can answer, the business case isn't finished.

Real example: executive program reporting

In a PMO running a portfolio of 20+ concurrent programs, I led a redesign of how status reached leadership. The result was 50% less executive reporting effort. Here's what changed in the process, not just the tools.

Before

  1. Status data pulled separately for two decks
  2. PMs build and update their slides
  3. Decks circulated to leadership weekly
  4. Leaders call PMs, often after hours, with questions

After

  1. One grounded data source: Planview AdaptiveWork
  2. Deck generated automatically from AdaptiveWork
  3. PMs edit one deck and fix any gaps in AdaptiveWork itself, so the source stays accurate
  4. Leaders self-serve any time: generate a leadership slide, open the initiative's AdaptiveWork page, or read the deck
  5. Weekly open review call for questions, feedback and walk-throughs with the PM

The next redesign: remove duplicate sources

Better visibility and faster decisions were the first payoff. The bigger value comes from the next step: redesigning processes that still keep the same information in two places. For example, AdaptiveWork already tracked issues and risks, but some PMs still kept their own individual RAID logs. Standardizing on one source would further improve accuracy, visibility and time savings, and the time saved could be redirected to new initiatives. That's the step from rung 2 to rungs 3 and 4.

Two improvements I'd make to the reporting itself:

  • A live dashboard grounded in AdaptiveWork instead of a once-a-week deck, so leaders can find any initiative instantly.
  • Automated capture from meeting minutes, pulling risks, action items and updates into the source. I prototyped this pattern in my AI status orchestrator: every item quotes the line it came from, and a person confirms it before it enters the log.

Redeploying the capacity

The plan for freed time is written before the automation goes live, agreed with the sponsor, and tracked afterward. This is the approach I'd use for a transformation office:

StepWhat happens
BaselineMeasure the hours each role spends on the process today, before anything changes.
Agree the destinationDecide with the sponsor where freed capacity goes: new initiatives, deeper risk and value analysis, stakeholder engagement, or a reduced backlog.
Redesign the rolesUpdate what each role is expected to do, with the people doing it. In a transformation office this is work for the workforce and change team, not just the PMO.
Track it as Value RiskIf capacity isn't being used as agreed after two cycles, it goes on the RAID log with an owner.
Report the business resultReport what the capacity produced, not just the hours saved.

Life Sciences example: promotional material review

Illustrative · a common pharma process, not a specific client

Before promotional and medical materials can be used, they go through review by medical, legal and regulatory teams (often called MLR review). It's a frequent bottleneck, and a good test of the difference between automating a task and redesigning a process.

Automating the task would have AI summarize reviewer comments faster. Helpful, but the material still goes around the same review loop, often more than once.

Redesigning the process moves the checking earlier, so reviewers spend their time on judgment:

Before

  1. Brand team drafts the material
  2. Submitted for review with missing or mismatched references
  3. Medical, legal and regulatory reviewers each comment
  4. Comments conflict or repeat; the author reconciles them by hand
  5. Revise and resubmit, often for several rounds
  6. Approved

After

  1. While drafting, AI checks claims against the approved claims library and flags missing references
  2. Author fixes issues before submitting
  3. Risk tier set by rules: minor updates take a fast-track route
  4. Reviewers focus on the judgment calls
  5. AI merges comments into one change list and flags conflicts for the reviewers to settle
  6. Reviewers approve. AI never approves.
Ladder stepIn this example
Redesign the processChecking moves to drafting; review is routed by risk; comments are consolidated
Redeploy the capacityReviewer time moves from catching missing references to higher-risk materials; brand teams get more materials to market
Measure the resultCycle time to approval, review rounds per piece, first-pass approval rate
ControlsPeople keep final approval; every AI flag and change is logged for audit

Where this shows up in my work

  • My operating rhythm toolkit and status orchestrator are rung 1 and 2 for program operations: assembly automated, the meeting turned into a decision forum.
  • My governance toolkit tracks the Value Risk, so a use case can't be called a success on hours saved alone.
  • In a new role, the redesign and redeployment plan is part of my first 90 days, not something added afterward.
